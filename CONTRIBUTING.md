# Contributing to AI-SDLC

AI-SDLC is an early working framework. Contributions are most valuable when they challenge the model with real engineering evidence.

## Useful contributions

We particularly welcome:

- field reports from real development teams,
- counterexamples to existing assumptions,
- proposals for R0–R4 classification and controls,
- review and testing patterns,
- engineering playbooks,
- examples of safe and unsafe AI delegation,
- measurable delivery or quality results,
- security, privacy and governance critiques,
- improvements to clarity and terminology.

## Contribution principles

1. **Evidence over hype.** Explain what was observed, not only what a tool promises.
2. **Human accountability remains.** Proposed automation should make ownership and decision authority explicit.
3. **Risk matters.** A practice that works for trivial work may be inappropriate for critical changes.
4. **Separate deterministic evidence from model judgement.** Prefer independently verifiable checks where possible.
5. **Preserve cognitive ownership.** Productivity improvements that remove engineering understanding are not automatically improvements.
6. **Keep the framework tool-agnostic where possible.** Tool-specific guidance belongs primarily in playbooks and adapters.

## Suggested field report format

When reporting an experiment, include where possible:

- context and team shape,
- type of change,
- assigned or estimated risk class,
- AI tooling/model used,
- what humans decided before delegation,
- what was delegated,
- validation and review controls,
- human interventions required,
- outcome,
- defects or rework,
- time/cost observations,
- what you would change next time.
