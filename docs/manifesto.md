# AI-SDLC Manifesto — v0.1

AI-SDLC is based on a simple premise:

> **Generative AI should augment engineering capability without transferring engineering responsibility away from humans.**

We recognize GenAI as a powerful execution, analysis and automation capability. We also recognize that software remains a human-created engineering system with consequences for users, organizations and society.

The objective is therefore not maximum automation. It is **responsible leverage**.

## 1. Augmentation over replacement

Use AI to reduce engineering toil, accelerate execution, improve analysis and increase the amount of verification that can be performed economically.

Do not use AI as a mechanism for removing human responsibility for understanding the problem or the resulting system.

## 2. Human ownership and accountability

Every material change must have an identifiable human owner or accountable team.

AI assistance does not transfer accountability.

> **AI may perform the work. A human must own the outcome.**

Ownership concerns the engineering outcome, not the percentage of an artifact manually authored by a person.

## 3. Cognitive ownership

Formal approval is not enough.

The responsible engineer must retain a sufficient mental model of the solution to explain what it does, why it works that way, what assumptions were made, how correctness was established and what may happen when it fails.

AI must not reduce engineers to passive reviewers of generated work.

The preferred sequence is:

**understand → design → define constraints → delegate → review**

not:

**delegate → inspect generated result → accept**

## 4. Human decision authority

AI may analyze, recommend, challenge and compare alternatives.

Material engineering decisions remain human decisions.

This includes decisions affecting architecture, domain behavior, contracts, data, security, privacy, consistency, concurrency, significant NFRs, production behavior and risk acceptance.

> **Recommendation is not authorization.**

Where AI encounters a material decision outside its delegated authority, the expected behavior is:

**STOP → DESCRIBE → PROPOSE → ESCALATE**

## 5. Bounded AI autonomy

AI autonomy is delegated, not assumed.

Within an approved boundary, AI should be allowed to perform real work autonomously. Outside that boundary, it must not silently expand scope.

Delegation may define:

- objective,
- context,
- allowed and forbidden scope,
- contracts and invariants,
- validation criteria,
- completion criteria,
- stop and escalation conditions.

Autonomy should be **real within the boundary and restrictive at the boundary**.

## 6. Risk-based autonomy

AI participation should depend on the risk and reversibility of the work.

AI-SDLC uses a working R0–R4 classification:

- **R0 — Trivial / Mechanical**
- **R1 — Standard**
- **R2 — Significant**
- **R3 — High Risk**
- **R4 — Critical**

> **Higher risk = narrower AI autonomy + stronger independent controls.**

Risk changes the required depth of analysis, design, review, testing, release safeguards, observability and recovery.

## 7. Human-defined correctness

AI should not define correctness merely by producing an implementation and then explaining why that implementation is correct.

Intent, constraints, contracts, acceptance scenarios and important test expectations should be established independently of generated implementation wherever practical.

The better humans define intent, constraints and correctness, the more safely execution can be delegated.

## 8. Independent validation

AI output is not trusted because it was generated by a capable model, passed an AI review or came from a successful autonomous run.

Generated and modified artifacts must pass controls appropriate to their nature and risk.

Deterministic checks, automated tests, independent review and human validation remain important because they provide evidence that is not simply another opinion from the same generation process.

## 9. Production remains accountable

Deployment automation may be extensive. Production accountability remains human.

Material GO / NO-GO, known-risk acceptance, rollback, roll-forward and release acceptance must remain attributable to an authorized human role or policy owned by humans.

AI-SDLC defines Done as:

> **Done = deployed, verified and operating correctly in production.**

## 10. Transparency and traceability

Material AI-assisted work should leave enough evidence to understand:

- what was delegated,
- which constraints applied,
- which assumptions or deviations appeared,
- which validations were performed,
- who accepted the resulting outcome.

Traceability exists to preserve accountability and learning, not to create compliance theatre.

## 11. Continuous evaluation

AI-SDLC is a living operating model.

Its value must be evaluated using delivery, quality and AI-specific evidence rather than assumed from tool capability or anecdotal speed.

A process that produces software faster while progressively removing system understanding, debugging ability, architectural knowledge or decision-making capability from the engineering team is not successful augmentation.

---

## The operating principle

**Human ownership always remains. AI participation expands where intent, constraints and validation become explicit — and contracts again where risk requires stronger human control.**
