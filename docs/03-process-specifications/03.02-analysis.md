# 03.02 Analysis

> **Baseline v1.** Tool-agnostic normative specification subject to later evaluation and change.

## 1. Intent

### Purpose

The purpose of **Analysis** is to transform an approved problem context into a sufficiently precise, testable and internally consistent functional specification.

Analysis defines **what the system is expected to do and under which conditions**, while avoiding premature commitment to implementation technology or architecture.

### Scope

**IN SCOPE**

* functional scope and boundaries,
* actors, roles and relevant permissions,
* business rules and domain terminology,
* functional flows and alternate flows,
* data expectations at a logical level,
* validations and error behavior,
* high-value edge cases,
* acceptance criteria and high-level acceptance scenarios,
* relevant NFR expectations that must be carried downstream,
* dependencies, assumptions, constraints and unresolved questions,
* traceability from problem to expected behavior.

**OUT OF SCOPE**

* final architecture,
* technology or framework selection,
* class-level implementation design,
* detailed task decomposition and estimation,
* concrete test-framework implementation.

### Inputs

Analysis SHOULD consume:

* approved Problem Statement,
* Discovery Record,
* assumptions and open questions,
* known constraints and dependencies,
* available domain or product knowledge,
* initial risk signals,
* existing-system behavior relevant to the change.

### Entry Criteria

Analysis MAY start when **Ready for Analysis** has been reached and:

* the problem and desired outcome are sufficiently understood,
* an accountable analyst/product role exists,
* material sources of business knowledge can be identified,
* blocking discovery contradictions are resolved or explicitly owned.

## 2. Responsibility & Authority

### Human Responsibilities

Humans MUST:

* own the interpretation of business intent,
* validate domain terminology and business rules,
* approve functional scope and exclusions,
* decide material ambiguity in requirements,
* approve acceptance criteria and critical scenarios,
* identify where NFR expectations are material,
* approve the final specification for downstream design.

### AI Responsibilities / Permitted Role

AI MAY:

* consolidate source information,
* identify gaps, contradictions and ambiguous wording,
* propose scenarios, alternate flows and edge cases,
* draft process, state or interaction diagrams,
* draft screen concepts or low-fidelity UI representations where useful,
* normalize terminology,
* generate traceability tables,
* compare the proposed behavior with available legacy documentation,
* challenge assumptions and completeness.

AI MUST NOT:

* invent business rules to close gaps,
* silently resolve conflicting stakeholder intent,
* approve scope or acceptance criteria,
* transform a proposed implementation into a functional requirement without human acceptance.

### Decision Rights

| Material Decision | Human Authority |
| --- | --- |
| Functional scope and exclusions | Product / Business / Analyst |
| Meaning of business rules | Business / Domain Owner |
| Acceptance criteria | Business / Product / Analyst |
| Material permissions/roles behavior | Product / Security / Domain Owner as applicable |
| NFR requirement significance | Lead / Architect / Security / Operations with Product as appropriate |
| Analysis approval | Accountable Product / Analyst / designated approver |

## 3. Execution Contract

### Logical Activities

**Review Discovery → Define Functional Scope → Model Actors / Rules / Flows → Identify Alternate & Failure Behavior → Define Acceptance Criteria → Capture Relevant NFR Expectations → Resolve Material Ambiguities → Validate Consistency & Traceability → Human Approval**

### Required Artifacts

At exit, the following logical artifacts MUST exist:

* **Functional Specification**,
* **Acceptance Criteria / High-Level Acceptance Scenarios**,
* **Assumptions / Open Questions Record**,
* **Known Constraints & Dependencies**.

Where applicable, Analysis SHOULD also produce logical process or state models, role/permission model, data concept model, UI behavior or wireframe concepts, initial NFR record and material decision records.

### Mandatory Artifact Content

The Functional Specification MUST define, where applicable:

* objective and scope,
* actors and relevant roles,
* normal business behavior,
* alternate and negative behavior,
* business rules and validations,
* expected errors and user/system responses,
* relevant logical data expectations,
* dependencies and constraints,
* explicit assumptions,
* acceptance criteria,
* material NFR expectations,
* unresolved non-blocking questions with owners.

Acceptance criteria MUST express externally meaningful behavior and MUST remain distinguishable from implementation detail.

## 4. Control

### Primary Gate — Analysis Approved

Analysis is complete when the expected system behavior is sufficiently clear, testable and approved to allow Architecture, Refinement and downstream design to proceed deliberately.

### Exit Criteria

The gate MUST confirm, proportionally to risk, that:

* functional scope and exclusions are explicit,
* critical business rules are understood,
* actors/permissions are sufficiently described,
* main happy and important negative paths are known,
* acceptance criteria are testable,
* material contradictions are resolved,
* relevant NFR expectations are visible,
* material dependencies and constraints are recorded,
* remaining questions are non-blocking and owned,
* traceability to the original problem is preserved,
* accountable humans approve the specification.

### Required Evidence

Evidence SHOULD include:

* approved Functional Specification,
* approved acceptance criteria/scenarios,
* current assumptions/open-questions record,
* decision evidence for material ambiguities.

### Risk Adjustments R0–R4

* **R0:** MAY use a compact behavior description and success criteria.
* **R1:** SHOULD follow the standard specification baseline.
* **R2:** SHOULD include explicit alternate flows, dependencies, role/permission behavior and initial NFR analysis.
* **R3:** MUST include stronger domain validation, risk-relevant edge cases and relevant specialist participation.
* **R4:** MUST use explicit, auditable acceptance criteria and involve required Security/Compliance/Data/Domain authorities before approval where applicable.

### Stop / Escalation Conditions

Escalate when stakeholder expectations materially conflict, acceptance behavior cannot be determined, required business authority is unavailable, a proposed rule creates material security/legal/privacy implications, analysis uncovers a materially higher risk class, or existing system behavior contradicts the intended requirement and the conflict cannot be locally resolved.

Use **STOP → DESCRIBE → PROPOSE → ESCALATE**.

### Exceptions

Emergency changes MAY use compressed Analysis, but MUST still define intended behavior, safety-critical acceptance criteria and accountable decision ownership. Missing detail SHOULD be reconstructed after stabilization if the change remains in the product.
