# 03.09 Review

> **Baseline v1.** Review is a layered validation process designed to preserve human ownership, independent reasoning and consistent engineering quality in AI-assisted development.

## 1. Intent

### Purpose

The purpose of **Review** is to establish independent confidence that the implementation matches the approved intent, respects architecture and risk controls, remains understandable to its Human Owner, and is suitable to proceed to formal Verification & Acceptance.

Review MUST validate more than code syntax or style. It must verify that the implementation is the right implementation of the intended solution.

### Scope

**IN SCOPE**

* author IDE review,
* AI-assisted review against approved contracts,
* CI quality gate before external human approval,
* independent human review,
* standardized review checklist / review book,
* pair review / engineering defence where required,
* review of material decisions, deviations and risks,
* final PR approval.

**OUT OF SCOPE**

* full business acceptance,
* complete regression testing,
* production release approval,
* rewriting the implementation without returning it to the appropriate owner/process stage.

### Inputs

Review SHOULD consume:

* approved Implementation Design / AI Implementation Contract,
* approved correctness contract / Test Intent,
* Source Change,
* automated test and quality results,
* Agent Completion Report where applicable,
* architecture and relevant decision records,
* current risk classification.

### Entry Criteria

Review MAY start when:

* Implementation is complete enough for meaningful inspection,
* the author has access to the full change and approved design context,
* required local validation is available,
* known material deviations have been surfaced rather than hidden.

## 2. Responsibility & Authority

### Human Responsibilities

The author MUST:

* review the AI-assisted change directly in the engineering environment,
* verify it against the design and correctness contract,
* understand and own the implementation,
* surface material uncertainty before external approval,
* be able to explain and defend material design/implementation decisions.

The independent reviewer MUST:

* form an independent view of the change,
* use the required review checklist proportionally to risk,
* challenge assumptions and hidden coupling,
* verify material decisions and deviations,
* approve, request changes or reject the change based on engineering evidence.

### AI Responsibilities / Permitted Role

AI MAY:

* compare implementation against the approved plan/contract,
* inspect correctness, architecture, error handling, security, concurrency, observability and maintainability,
* identify unexpected scope expansion,
* inspect test adequacy and missing cases,
* summarize diffs and material risk areas,
* support review discussion.

AI MUST NOT:

* become the sole reviewer for material work,
* replace independent human reasoning,
* approve its own implementation as a substitute for human accountability,
* hide uncertainty behind a generic “looks good” result.

### Decision Rights

| Material Decision | Human Authority |
| --- | --- |
| Whether author understanding is sufficient | Author + Reviewer / Lead where applicable |
| Whether review finding is blocking | Reviewer / Lead according to risk |
| Acceptance of material deviation | Appropriate Human Decision Owner |
| Requirement/architecture change discovered in review | Return to appropriate upstream owner |
| PR approval | Authorized Human Reviewer(s) |

## 3. Execution Contract

### Logical Activities

**Author IDE Review → AI Review Against Contract → CI / Quality Gate → Independent Human Review → Review Findings → Pair Review / Defence where required → Reviewer Re-check → PR Approval or Return for Changes**

For independent review, AI-SDLC SHOULD prefer:

**Reviewer independent inspection → reviewer initial findings → author explanation / defence → discussion → reviewer final pass**

rather than exposing the reviewer first to a detailed author/AI narrative. This reduces anchoring.

### Required Artifacts

At exit, logical artifacts SHOULD include:

* **Reviewed Source Change / PR Candidate**,
* **Automated Quality Evidence**,
* [**AI Review Findings**](../04-playbooks/review/output-ai-review-findings.md) where AI review is used,
* [**Human Review Record / Checklist Evidence**](../04-playbooks/review/output-human-review-record.md),
* [**Review Decision Record**](../04-playbooks/review/output-review-decision-record.md) or equivalent source-control approval/change-request evidence.

For higher-risk work, [**Pair Review & Defence Record**](../04-playbooks/review/output-pair-review-defence-record.md) MAY also be required.

These are **logical evidence contracts**, not mandatory standalone documents for every change. Native PR, CI and review-system evidence MAY satisfy the contract when it preserves equivalent information required by the assigned risk class.

### Mandatory Review Dimensions

The standardized review model SHOULD cover, where applicable:

* scope and contract compliance,
* domain invariants,
* architecture compliance,
* error and failure handling,
* transactionality and consistency,
* security and permissions,
* concurrency,
* persistence/data handling,
* observability,
* test adequacy,
* maintainability/readability,
* performance implications,
* unexpected dependencies or scope changes,
* AI-generated assumptions/deviations.

Technology-specific review dimensions belong to L4 playbooks.

### Pair Review / Defence

Where required by risk, the author SHOULD explain:

* what changed,
* why the selected approach was chosen,
* alternatives/trade-offs considered,
* material AI-made local decisions,
* key failure modes,
* how correctness was validated.

If the author cannot adequately explain a material change, the PR MUST NOT be approved solely because tests and AI review are green.

## 4. Control

### Primary Gate — PR Approved

Review is complete when the implementation has passed required automated and human review layers and an authorized human reviewer accepts the change.

### Exit Criteria

The gate MUST confirm, proportionally to risk, that:

* the author has reviewed and understands the implementation,
* required CI/quality gates are green,
* implementation matches approved scope and contracts,
* material deviations are explicit and accepted,
* independent human review has occurred where required,
* blocking findings are resolved,
* reviewer independence has been preserved sufficiently,
* the change is ready for merge and formal acceptance validation.

### Required Evidence

Evidence SHOULD include:

* source diff / PR,
* CI and static-analysis results,
* [AI Review Findings](../04-playbooks/review/output-ai-review-findings.md) where applicable,
* [Human Review Record](../04-playbooks/review/output-human-review-record.md) or equivalent checklist/review evidence,
* approval identity and timestamp through the selected tool/process,
* [Pair Review & Defence Record](../04-playbooks/review/output-pair-review-defence-record.md) for higher-risk work where required,
* [Review Decision Record](../04-playbooks/review/output-review-decision-record.md) where native tooling does not preserve sufficient final-decision evidence.

### Risk Adjustments R0–R4

* **R0:** MAY use lightweight author review plus automated checks; independent review MAY be waived by policy.
* **R1:** SHOULD use standard human review and automated quality gates.
* **R2:** MUST include independent human review; pair review/defence SHOULD be used for material AI-generated implementation.
* **R3:** MUST use strong independent review, explicit checklist evidence and lead/specialist involvement where risk requires it.
* **R4:** MUST use multi-party/specialist review, explicit human ownership and strongly independent validation. AI review is supplemental only.

### Stop / Escalation Conditions

Review MUST stop or return upstream when:

* implementation differs materially from approved intent,
* author cannot explain a material change,
* hidden architecture/security/data changes are discovered,
* test intent appears to have been changed to fit implementation,
* risk class increases,
* a Material Decision lacks authorized approval,
* reviewer confidence depends only on the implementing AI's explanation.

### Exceptions

Low-risk changes MAY use reduced review controls according to the R0–R4 model. Emergency changes MAY use break-glass review, but MUST preserve accountable approval, minimum independent verification and post-action review where required.

## 5. Traceability & Navigation

### Traceability

**Implementation Contract → Source Change → Agent Completion Report → Author Review → AI/Tooling Evidence → Independent Human Review → PR Approval**

### Upstream / Downstream Contracts

**Consumes:** 03.08 Implementation and its upstream design/test contracts.

**Produces for:** merge and 03.10 Verification & Acceptance.
