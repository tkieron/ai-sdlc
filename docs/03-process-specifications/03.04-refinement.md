# 03.04 Refinement

> **Baseline v1.** Refinement is team-owned and primarily human-led; AI may support consolidation, gap detection and documentation.

## 1. Intent

### Purpose

The purpose of **Refinement** is to establish shared team understanding of the approved functional and architectural intent, remove material ambiguity, expose dependencies and risks, and prepare the work for deliberate Planning.

Refinement is not a second Analysis phase. It is the point at which the delivery team validates that the specification can be understood and implemented without hidden assumptions.

### Scope

**IN SCOPE**

* review of approved functional and architectural intent,
* clarification of ambiguous behavior,
* identification of implementation-relevant edge cases,
* confirmation of dependencies and external owners,
* clarification of scope and explicit non-scope,
* identification of risks discovered by the delivery team,
* validation of assumptions against current system knowledge,
* identification of missing acceptance or NFR information,
* preparation of input for Planning and Test Design.

**OUT OF SCOPE**

* final implementation decomposition,
* estimation commitment,
* detailed class/method design,
* agent implementation execution,
* detailed test automation design.

### Inputs

Refinement SHOULD consume:

* approved Functional Specification,
* approved Architecture Design / material ADRs,
* current acceptance criteria/scenarios,
* risk classification,
* known dependencies and constraints,
* relevant current-system knowledge.

### Entry Criteria

Refinement MAY start when:

* functional intent is approved or sufficiently stable,
* material architecture constraints are available,
* the delivery team can access the required context,
* critical stakeholders/owners are identifiable for unresolved questions.

## 2. Responsibility & Authority

### Human Responsibilities

The team MUST:

* validate shared understanding of the intended behavior,
* surface hidden assumptions and contradictions,
* identify implementation-relevant risks and dependencies,
* clarify whether work remains within the approved scope,
* identify information missing for Planning or Test Design,
* approve the refined result.

The Tech Lead / accountable delivery owner SHOULD ensure that unresolved material issues are not silently carried forward.

### AI Responsibilities / Permitted Role

AI MAY:

* summarize refinement discussions and recordings,
* compare discussion outcomes against existing documentation,
* identify gaps, contradictions or missing edge cases,
* propose structured questions,
* update logical documentation artifacts after human decisions,
* classify open questions and dependencies,
* prepare draft refinement summaries.

AI MUST NOT:

* convert tentative discussion into approved scope without human confirmation,
* silently resolve disagreement between team and specification,
* introduce new architecture or functional behavior as an implementation shortcut,
* mark unresolved material questions as closed without evidence.

### Decision Rights

| Material Decision | Human Authority |
| --- | --- |
| Clarification of business behavior | Product / Analyst / Domain Owner |
| Clarification of architecture constraints | Architect / Tech Lead |
| Confirmation of delivery scope | Product / Delivery Owner / Team |
| Acceptance of newly discovered risk | Appropriate Human Risk Owner |
| Refinement completion | Team / Tech Lead / Product as locally defined |

## 3. Execution Contract

### Logical Activities

**Review Approved Context → Walk Through Main Behaviors → Challenge Assumptions → Identify Edge Cases / Dependencies / Risks → Resolve or Assign Open Questions → Confirm Scope → Update Artifacts → Team Validation → Ready for Planning**

### Required Artifacts

At exit, the following logical outputs MUST be available:

* **Refined Requirement Context**,
* **Updated Open Questions / Assumptions**,
* **Known Dependencies & Owners**,
* **Updated Risk Notes**.

Where needed, Refinement SHOULD also update acceptance scenarios, scope / out-of-scope boundaries, architecture decision references and NFR concerns.

### Mandatory Artifact Content

The refined context MUST make clear, where applicable:

* what behavior is understood and agreed,
* what remains explicitly out of scope,
* which dependencies may affect delivery,
* which edge cases require downstream treatment,
* which assumptions remain active,
* which questions remain open and who owns them,
* whether the risk class or required controls need reassessment.

## 4. Control

### Local Gate — Refinement Complete / Ready for Planning

Refinement is complete when the team has enough shared understanding to decompose and estimate the work without inventing missing business or architectural decisions during Planning.

### Exit Criteria

The gate SHOULD confirm, proportionally to risk, that:

* the team can explain the intended behavior,
* critical ambiguity has been resolved or explicitly owned,
* important dependencies are known,
* material edge cases are visible,
* scope and non-scope are sufficiently clear,
* no hidden architecture contradiction is knowingly ignored,
* risk classification has been reconsidered if new information emerged,
* Planning and Test Design can proceed without guessing material intent.

### Required Evidence

Evidence MAY include updated refined requirements, refinement notes / decision summary, current open-question and dependency list and explicit team/lead approval where required by risk.

### Risk Adjustments R0–R4

* **R0:** MAY be satisfied asynchronously with lightweight human confirmation.
* **R1:** SHOULD use standard team refinement.
* **R2:** SHOULD explicitly review dependencies, failure behavior and risk-relevant edge cases.
* **R3:** MUST involve relevant technical/specialist owners for material risks and SHOULD preserve explicit refinement evidence.
* **R4:** MUST NOT proceed while material ambiguity exists in critical behavior, security, data or operational constraints.

### Stop / Escalation Conditions

Escalate when specification and architecture materially conflict, the team cannot explain key expected behavior, a dependency owner is unavailable for a blocking contract decision, new information materially changes scope or risk, required acceptance behavior is missing, or the team discovers a Material Decision not yet owned by an authorized human.

Use **STOP → DESCRIBE → PROPOSE → ESCALATE**.

### Exceptions

Low-risk, well-understood work MAY use lightweight or asynchronous refinement. The simplification MUST NOT result in hidden ambiguity being transferred into implementation.
