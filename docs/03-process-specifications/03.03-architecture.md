# 03.03 Architecture

> **Baseline v1.** Architecture is intentionally **human-owned · AI consultative**.

## 1. Intent

### Purpose

The purpose of **Architecture** is to define the material structural and cross-cutting decisions required for the solution to satisfy its functional and non-functional intent safely and sustainably.

Architecture determines the important **system-level HOW**, while leaving local implementation detail to later stages.

### Scope

**IN SCOPE**

* system and component boundaries,
* domain and bounded-context implications where material,
* interaction and integration patterns,
* public/internal contracts at architectural level,
* data ownership and consistency strategy,
* transaction and concurrency boundaries,
* security and privacy architecture,
* relevant resilience, scalability and performance approaches,
* observability and operational implications,
* deployment and migration constraints,
* material technology choices and trade-offs,
* Architecture / Decision Records.

**OUT OF SCOPE**

* private helper design,
* detailed class structure,
* method-level implementation,
* framework-specific coding conventions,
* final task decomposition.

### Inputs

Architecture SHOULD consume:

* approved Functional Specification,
* acceptance criteria/scenarios,
* known NFR expectations,
* current system architecture and constraints,
* relevant existing contracts and dependencies,
* risk classification,
* security/privacy/legal constraints.

### Entry Criteria

Architecture MAY start when:

* Analysis is approved or sufficiently mature for architectural decisions,
* material functional behavior is understood,
* known architectural constraints are available,
* a competent Human Decision Owner is identified.

## 2. Responsibility & Authority

### Human Responsibilities

Humans MUST:

* own material architectural decisions,
* understand and approve domain/system boundaries,
* decide material trade-offs,
* approve public contracts and data ownership implications,
* define or approve consistency/transaction strategies,
* approve material security and operational implications,
* ensure architecture is proportionate to risk and problem complexity.

### AI Responsibilities / Permitted Role

AI MAY:

* challenge assumptions,
* compare architectural alternatives,
* identify coupling, scaling, security or resilience risks,
* propose architecture views and diagrams,
* analyze consistency between requirements and architecture,
* summarize trade-offs,
* draft ADRs after human decisions,
* identify missing NFR implications,
* search available internal knowledge for relevant patterns.

AI MUST NOT:

* autonomously approve architecture,
* silently redefine domain or service boundaries,
* introduce material public contracts or persistence changes without human decision,
* treat a recommended pattern as authorization to implement it.

### Decision Rights

| Material Decision | Human Authority |
| --- | --- |
| Domain / bounded-context boundaries | Architect / Tech Lead / Domain Owner |
| System/component boundaries | Architect / Tech Lead |
| Public API/event contracts | Architect / Lead + relevant consumers/owners |
| Data ownership and consistency | Architect / Lead / Data Owner |
| Security architecture | Security Owner / Architect |
| Material technology choice | Architect / Lead / Engineering governance |
| Production architecture / deployment implications | Architect / Platform / Operations owner |
| Architecture approval | Designated Architect / Tech Lead / architecture authority |

## 3. Execution Contract

### Logical Activities

**Review Functional Intent & Constraints → Identify Material Architectural Decisions → Evaluate Alternatives & Trade-offs → Define Boundaries / Contracts / Data & Interaction Model → Address NFR & Operational Concerns → Record Decisions → Validate Against Requirements & Risk → Human Approval**

### Required Artifacts

At exit, logical artifacts MUST include, where material:

* **Architecture Design**,
* **Material Decision Records / ADRs**,
* **Architectural Contracts / Boundaries**,
* **NFR & Operational Implications**,
* **Known Architectural Risks / Assumptions**.

### Mandatory Artifact Content

Architecture Design MUST define, where applicable:

* system context and affected boundaries,
* relevant components/services and responsibilities,
* interaction patterns,
* public/internal contracts that constrain implementation,
* data ownership and persistence implications,
* consistency and transaction model,
* relevant concurrency behavior,
* security/privacy architecture,
* resilience/performance/scalability assumptions,
* observability expectations,
* deployment/migration implications,
* material trade-offs and rejected alternatives,
* unresolved architectural risks with owners.

Material decisions SHOULD be recorded separately when future teams would need to understand **why** the decision was made.

## 4. Control

### Local Gate — Architecture Approved / Sufficient for Downstream Design

Architecture is complete when all material architectural decisions required for refinement, planning, test design and implementation design are sufficiently explicit and human-approved.

### Exit Criteria

The gate MUST confirm, proportionally to risk, that:

* material system boundaries are understood,
* contracts constraining downstream work are explicit,
* data ownership/consistency decisions are sufficient,
* material security and NFR implications are addressed,
* key trade-offs are conscious rather than accidental,
* material architectural risks are visible,
* no unresolved architecture question blocks implementation planning,
* required Human Decision Owners have approved material decisions.

### Required Evidence

Evidence SHOULD include:

* approved Architecture Design,
* relevant ADRs / Decision Records,
* identified architectural risks,
* approval evidence appropriate to the risk class.

### Risk Adjustments R0–R4

* **R0:** MAY require no separate architecture artifact if the change is purely local and respects existing architecture.
* **R1:** SHOULD document only material deviations or new decisions.
* **R2:** SHOULD explicitly document affected boundaries, contracts, data/consistency and NFR implications.
* **R3:** MUST include formal human architecture approval and relevant specialist input where security/data/operations are materially affected.
* **R4:** MUST use explicit, auditable decisions, specialist review and strongly bounded AI participation. Critical irreversible decisions MUST NOT be delegated to AI.

### Stop / Escalation Conditions

Escalate when:

* architecture would contradict approved functional intent,
* a new Material Decision appears outside current authority,
* risk class increases materially,
* security/privacy/legal implications are unresolved,
* proposed migration or data change is not safely recoverable,
* required dependent-system owners cannot agree on a public contract,
* no competent Human Decision Owner is available.

### Exceptions

Existing standard architecture MAY be reused without new documentation when no material decision is introduced. The absence of a new diagram or ADR does not remove the requirement to understand and respect existing architectural constraints.
